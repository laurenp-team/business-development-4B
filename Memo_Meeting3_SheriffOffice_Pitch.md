## Connected Research

- **CJIS compliance certification nuance**: there is no official "CJIS certification" a vendor can hold — no vendor can certify an agency as compliant, and a vendor claiming to be "CJIS Certified" outright can be a red flag. What actually matters: signing the CJIS Security Addendum, completing risk assessments, security awareness training, and holding recognized credentials (CISSP, CISA, CISM, GSNA). Version 6.0 of the CJIS Security Policy took effect December 27, 2024; Version 6.1 is expected spring 2026. Connects to the Procurement Officer's specific requirement (CJIS compliance) and Dave's compliance questions. Confirmed fact — a documented policy framework. **This directly affects what we send**: compliance materials should avoid claiming a blanket "CJIS certification" and instead cite the Security Addendum, risk assessments, and credentials, reflecting Version 6.0.
- **CJIS disaster recovery requirements**: requires documented DR/business continuity plans with clear roles, identified critical assets, tested and regularly updated contingency plans, and (scale-dependent) a physically separate alternate site. Connects directly to Dave's questions about uptime guarantees and disaster recovery. Confirmed fact — a documented policy requirement that maps directly to what the technical deep-dive materials should cover.
